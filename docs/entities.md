## How to read this page

* **Name** is the name on the device page. Home Assistant puts the device name in front, for example "Heat pump Heating Setpoint".
* **Key** is the stable identifier. The unique ID is `<entry_id>_<key>` and never changes; the entity ID is derived from device and name when the entity is first created. Below the key: the platform, the Modbus table with the register address, and the device it sits in.
* **Range / values** shows the write range of numbers, the options of selects and enumerations (raw number, key, shown text) and the meaning of raw numbers.
* **Sources** at the end of an explanation: **PL** python-luxtronik (Bouni/python-luxtronik, `luxtronik/definitions/{inputs,holdings}.py`, `datatypes.py`); **FW** [firmware analysis](firmware-3.92.3-shi-analysis.md) and [register meanings](firmware-3.92.3-register-meanings.md) of V3.92.3 (English); **M** measurement on the reference installation (LWDV 91-1/3, firmware 3.92.3), documented in the README or CHANGELOG; **OBS** observation only, no written source; **INT** decision or calculation of this integration; **WEB** general background, see the [sources](#sources) below.
* The notes in italics below an explanation are generated from the code: disabled by default, category, needed equipment, polling rate, restore behaviour.
* Registers are polled in tiers: the base interval (default 30 s) for measurements, every Nth interval for slow values; a write is read back at once.
* A value the controller reports as *not available* is the raw number 32767 (0x7FFF), see the pitfalls.

## 1. Temperatures of the heating circuit

**Background.** A heat pump warms the water that runs through the heating system (the *flow*, German *Vorlauf*) and gets it back cooler (the *return*, *Rücklauf*). The difference between the two is the *spread* (*Spreizung*). The integration has no spread entity; flow minus return in a template sensor gives it. The Luxtronik controller regulates on the **return** temperature: the heating setpoint of the SHI acts on it (see [Heating](#8-heating-circuit-control)). Its normal target comes from the *heating curve* (*Heizkurve*), which assigns a water temperature to every outside temperature: the *slope* says how strongly the water temperature follows the outside temperature, the *level* (or offset) shifts the whole curve up or down. Above the *heating limit* (*Heizgrenze*, an outside temperature) a heating curve controller stops heating. The lower the water temperature a heat pump has to deliver, the more efficient it is: the German Wikipedia article on heat pumps gives the example of a COP falling from 4.8 (B0/W35) to 3.6 (B0/W50).

**Pitfall: return and return target.** The published register list names 10100 *return target* and 10101 *return actual*. Measured on the reference installation it is the other way round, and the entities follow the measurement (`return` = 10100 follows the water, `return_target` = 10101 is the target). python-luxtronik agrees. Sources: PL, M, WEB ([Heizkurve](#sources), [Wärmepumpe](#sources)).

## 2. Hot water temperatures

**Background.** Hot water is stored in a tank and heated up to a target. Like every two-point control it uses a *hysteresis* (*Schaltdifferenz*): the heat pump only starts again after the temperature has dropped a few kelvin below the target, otherwise it would switch on and off all the time. The SHI does not expose that value. **Legionella:** the German Umweltbundesamt recommends keeping the hot water permanently above 55 °C; for large systems DVGW W 551 requires at least 60 °C at the outlet of the heater. A heat pump with a lower hot water target depends on periodic heating above that (a *Legionellenschaltung*), which the UBA does not consider a safe variant on its own. This integration has no entity for such a function; the register map contains none. Sources: WEB ([UBA factsheet](#sources), [Zweipunktregler](#sources)).

## 3. Temperatures of the mixing circuits

**Background.** A *mixing circuit* (*Mischkreis*) has its own flow temperature, produced by a mixing valve that blends hot flow water with cooler return water. Typical use: a floor heating circuit that needs less than the radiator circuit. The SHI only provides temperatures, modes and setpoints of the circuits, no valve position. The circuits exist only if they are selected in the [equipment](../README.md#installation-equipment); circuits 2 and 3 are disabled by default. Sources: PL, INT.

## 4. Power and calculated COP

**Background.** The *COP* (*Leistungszahl*) is the heat output divided by the electrical power used at the same moment. It is always above 1, because the heat includes what is taken from the environment, and it depends on the operating point: a small difference between source and heating water gives a high COP, a large one a low COP. A COP of 4.2 means 1 kW of electricity delivers 4.2 kW of heat. The COP sensor of this integration is calculated from two sensors that are read anyway, so it needs no extra Modbus traffic, and it is a value over the last 10 minutes, not an instant value. Both power registers have a **resolution of 100 W**, so a quotient at low power is mostly rounding noise; that is why the COP is shown as *unknown* instead of a number in the cases listed at the sensor. During **defrost** the heat pump takes heat from the heating system and the thermal power turns negative. Sources: WEB ([Leistungszahl](#sources)), INT, PL.

| Power: electric | `electric_power`<br>sensor · input 10301 · device Heat pump | W | - | Current electrical power consumption. Resolution 100 W. Whether it includes circulation pumps and the additional heater is not documented (open). *(PL)* |
| Power: electric minimum | `electric_power_min`<br>sensor · input 10302 · device Heat pump | W | - | Minimum predicted electrical power consumption. How the controller predicts it and for which state is not documented (open). *(PL)*<br>*category Diagnostic; read every 10 polls* |

Shown as *unknown* while the window holds too little electrical energy (compressor off or just started), after a gap in the readings, and when the result is not plausible, which includes a net thermal energy that is not positive (a long defrost). Defrost inside a run counts, it is real consumption. The window and limits are a design choice of the integration, not a register fact. *(INT)* |

## 5. Energy counters, operating hours and overall COP

**Background.** The counters only ever go up (Home Assistant statistics class *total increasing*) and are meant for the Energy dashboard. Heat divided by electricity over a full year is the *annual performance factor* (*Jahresarbeitszahl*, JAZ). **The overall COP sensors of this integration are not a JAZ:** they divide the lifetime counters of the controller, so they average the whole life of the counters, not one year. For a JAZ take the change of both counters over a year from the Home Assistant statistics. Also note the system boundary: the usual JAZ definition leaves out auxiliary loads such as circulation pumps and electric defrost heaters. Whether the electrical counters of the controller include the auxiliary heater, and whether *total* equals the sum of the parts, is not documented (open). Sources: WEB ([Arbeitszahl](#sources), [Leistungszahl](#sources)), PL, INT.

## 6. Status and operating state

**Background.** *VD1* and *VD2* are the controller's names for the compressors (*Verdichter*). *ZWE1* to *ZWE3* are additional heat generators, for example an electric heating element; direct electric heating has a COP of 1, so a running additional heater costs efficiency. On air-to-water heat pumps the evaporator ices up in cold, humid weather. The heat pump then **defrosts** by reversing the refrigerant circuit for a short time; the heat for it comes from the heating system, which is why the thermal power is negative and *operating mode* shows *defrost*. A **utility lockout** (*EVU-Sperre*) is a block by the grid operator for heat pumps on a special tariff; a supplier may switch off up to three times a day for up to two hours each. Heating, hot water, cooling and pool take turns: the *operating mode* is a single value and names only what the heat pump does right now. Sources: WEB ([Wärmepumpe](#sources), [EVU-Sperre](#sources)), PL.

## 7. Blocks (switches)

**Background.** A *block* (*Sperre*) stops one function of the heat pump while the rest keeps running. Blocks are the tool of an optimizer such as the Heat Pump Optimizer: it blocks heating or hot water while electricity is expensive and releases them again. They are **volatile**: after a controller reboot or when the SHI watchdog expires (10 minutes without any Modbus access) they read *off* (not blocked), which is the safe state. The restore option of this integration deliberately does **not** restore blocks. Frequent switching of cooling or pool blocks may wear heat pump and hydraulic components (PL). Sources: PL, FW, INT.

## 8. Heating circuit control

**Background: the four control modes.** The SHI does not replace the controller, it lays a correction on top of it. Every circuit has a *mode* (raw 0 to 3) that decides which of the three companion values counts:

| Raw | Key | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `off` | **No influence**: the controller uses its own values. This does **not** switch the circuit off, heating goes on. |
| 1 | `setpoint` | The *setpoint* number replaces the controller's target. |
| 2 | `offset` | The controller's target plus the *offset* in kelvin. |
| 3 | `level` | The controller's target corrected by the *level*. |

**The heating setpoint is a return temperature setpoint (tRL)**, not a flow target (PL, M). It applies **only in mode `setpoint`**; in every other mode the controller ignores it. The numbers still accept a write in any mode, because optimizers write the number and set the mode themselves; the *climate* entity refuses a target in another mode and names the mode select instead. The attributes `setpoint_active` and `effective_target` show whether the setpoint counts and what the controller really aims at. The heating curve itself (slope, level) is not accessible through the SHI; the *offset* is the SHI's way to shift the result. The value ranges are those of python-luxtronik; the controller applies its own limits on top. **Pitfall:** after a controller reboot or a watchdog expiry modes and levels read 0 and setpoints fall back to a stored or default value (not 0). The option *restore after a device reset* writes the values it set itself back (see [pitfalls](#pitfalls-and-things-to-know)). Do not let an optimizer and the climate entity write the same circuit at the same time. Sources: PL, FW, M, INT.

## 9. Hot water control

**Background.** Hot water has the same four control modes as the heating circuit (see [Heating circuit control](#8-heating-circuit-control)); the hot water setpoint is a plain hot water temperature and only counts in mode `setpoint`. **Pitfalls specific to hot water:**

* **Deferred commit.** On firmware 3.92.3 writes to mode, setpoint, offset and level of hot water do not take effect at once; the controller commits them after about 10 s (inferred from the firmware, FW). The integration therefore checks a differing read-back again after 15 s and writes nothing to that register meanwhile. Writing the value that is currently stored cancels a pending write. Measured for the mode; the setpoint read back at once on the reference installation, so firmware and measurement disagree there (FW, M).
* **Hot water block.** The owner measured that the hot water mode can only be set while the hot water block is not active; the mechanism is unknown (M). The restore after a reset therefore waits while the hot water block reads on.
* The `water_heater` entity shows the mode as *no influence / setpoint / offset / level* and refuses a target outside mode `setpoint`.

## 12. Power limit

**Background.** The power limit (*LPC*, limitation of power consumption) caps the electrical power of the heat pump. *Soft* is a recommendation that is meant for PV surplus: the heat pump tries to stay below the value, but ignores it if the actual temperature deviates too far from the target (outside its hysteresis). *Hard* limits the consumption regardless of the hysteresis and may reduce comfort; python-luxtronik recommends it only for §14a EnWG. §14a EnWG lets the grid operator reduce controllable consumers such as heat pumps; the lower bound is 4.2 kW (per device). Both entities are deliberately not restored after a reset: an optimizer owns them. Sources: PL, WEB ([§14a EnWG](#sources)).

## 14. System, firmware and equipment

**Background: buffer tank.** A *buffer tank* (*Pufferspeicher*) holds heating water between heat pump and heating circuits. A **series buffer** (*Reihenpuffer*) sits in the return and is flowed through by all of the water, so practically nothing is lost; it evens out the flow and gives a reserve for defrosting. A **separation buffer** (*Trennpuffer*) decouples the heat pump circuit from the heating circuits with separate pumps; flow and return mix a little in the tank, which costs about 2 to 5 K and with it some efficiency. A **multi-function buffer** combines more functions in one tank. Because a heat pump should not switch on and off too often, the controller also keeps a *minimum runtime* and a *switching lock* (minimum off time); on the reference installation the firmware returns both as constants (20 minutes, FW). The **equipment flags** (`cap_*`) are discrete inputs that tell which parts the installation has; the setup dialog uses them to pre-select the equipment. The firmware version is read from three registers and is only informational. Sources: PL, FW, OBS, WEB ([Reihenpuffer und Trennpuffer](#sources)).

## Pitfalls and things to know

1. **Values set over the SHI are volatile.** The controller discards every value set over Modbus when it reboots, and when nothing has accessed the SHI for 600 s (10 minutes); every Modbus access resets that timer, a pure read included. While the integration polls, the watchdog does not expire; the maximum update interval is therefore 300 s. After a reset **modes, levels and blocks read 0**, **setpoints read their stored or default value** (not 0), and offsets are assumed to read 0 (not proven). A Home Assistant downtime of more than 10 minutes has the same effect. Blocks reading 0 means *not blocked*: the heat pump falls back to its own control (FW).
2. **Restore after a device reset** is an option, off by default. When on, the integration writes back the values it set itself, but only after a connection outage and only if every one of them reads its reset value; a change seen during continuous polling counts as somebody else's. Restored keys: `cfg_dhw_level`, `cfg_dhw_mode`, `cfg_dhw_offset`, `cfg_dhw_setpoint`, `cfg_heating_level`, `cfg_heating_mode`, `cfg_heating_offset`, `cfg_heating_setpoint`, `cfg_mk1_cool_mode`, `cfg_mk1_cool_offset`, `cfg_mk1_cool_setpoint`, `cfg_mk1_level`, `cfg_mk1_mode`, `cfg_mk1_offset`, `cfg_mk1_setpoint`, `cfg_mk2_cool_mode`, `cfg_mk2_cool_offset`, `cfg_mk2_cool_setpoint`, `cfg_mk2_level`, `cfg_mk2_mode`, `cfg_mk2_offset`, `cfg_mk2_setpoint`, `cfg_mk3_cool_mode`, `cfg_mk3_cool_offset`, `cfg_mk3_cool_setpoint`, `cfg_mk3_level`, `cfg_mk3_mode`, `cfg_mk3_offset`, `cfg_mk3_setpoint`, `cfg_room_setpoint`. Blocks, circulation pump, extra hot water, the power limit and the overall group are deliberately left out. Leave the option off if another system also writes these registers.
3. **A setpoint only counts in its mode `setpoint`.** In `off`, `offset` and `level` the controller ignores it. The setpoint numbers still write in every mode; climate and water heater refuse. Check `setpoint_active` and `effective_target`.
4. **Mode `off` is "no influence", not off.** To stop heating use the heating block; to stop hot water the hot water block.
5. **Hot water writes are committed late** (about 10 s) and not while the hot water block is on, see [hot water control](#9-hot-water-control).
6. **Two writers overwrite each other.** The Heat Pump Optimizer, OpenEMS, the native Luxtronik integration (port 8889), the controller panel and this integration write the same settings; the last write wins. A change that did not come from Home Assistant raises the repair issue *Value changed outside Home Assistant*; nothing is written back.
7. **Not available.** A register the controller reports as unavailable reads the raw number 32767 (0x7FFF), for example 3276.7 on a temperature sensor. The integration does not translate this value; treat it as unknown. A register that does not exist at all makes the entity *unknown*.
8. **Equipment decides which entities exist.** Parts that are switched off in the options are not polled and get no entities; their entity IDs stay reserved. Do not switch off a part that an optimizer reads or writes (for example mixing circuit 1).
9. **Write ranges** are those of python-luxtronik. The controller applies its own limits on top; the read-back after every write shows what it really took.
10. **Slow values** (counters, hours, settings, firmware) are read only every Nth poll, so they can lag by several update intervals.
